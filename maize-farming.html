01 / Introduction

Why Maize Farming Matters

Maize is one of Africa's most important food and cash crops. It supports household food security, livestock feed production, agricultural trade and rural employment.

In Rwanda, maize is grown in different agroecological conditions. Successful production depends on selecting varieties suited to the location, planting at the right time and maintaining healthy soil.

High yields result from a combination of quality seed, suitable field preparation, balanced nutrition, timely weed management, pest monitoring and proper post-harvest handling.

Key principle: Plan the whole season before planting. Seed choice, input costs, labor, rainfall expectations and market opportunities all matter.
Maize production connects farming, food security and agribusiness.
02 / Production Process

Six Essential Stages of Maize Farming

Follow these stages to establish a healthy crop and improve your chances of a successful harvest. Recommendations should be adapted to local conditions and the selected maize variety.

01

Choose Suitable Land

Select fertile, well-drained soil with adequate sunlight and good moisture availability.

  • Avoid fields prone to prolonged waterlogging.
  • Check soil depth, slope and drainage.
  • Use soil tests to guide amendments.

03 / Field Establishment

Land Selection & Soil Preparation

Build the Foundation for Healthy Roots

Maize needs soil that allows roots to develop and absorb water and nutrients efficiently. Poor drainage, severe compaction and low fertility can reduce crop performance.

Where possible, test the soil before planting. This helps determine whether lime, organic matter or fertilizer is needed. On sloping land, protect the field from erosion.

  • Select deep, fertile and well-drained soil.
  • Manage previous crop residues responsibly.
  • Use contour farming or other erosion-control practices where appropriate.
  • Avoid unnecessary tillage that damages soil structure.
04 / Seed & Planting

Choose the Right Variety and Plant Correctly

Quality Seed Is an Investment

Choose certified maize seed suited to your growing environment. Compare maturity period, yield potential, tolerance to local stresses and intended market.

A commonly used example is 75 cm between rows and 25–30 cm between planting positions, with planting depth around 3–5 cm. Actual recommendations depend on the variety, number of plants per position and local conditions.

  • Plant when adequate soil moisture is available.
  • Follow the seed supplier's population and spacing guidance.
  • Use consistent planting depth for even emergence.
  • Inspect germination shortly after planting.
05 / Crop Management

Nutrition, Weeding & Pest Protection

Once the maize has emerged, consistent management is essential. Small problems can become expensive when they are not detected early.

Keep the Crop Healthy

Fertilizer recommendations should be based on soil tests, crop needs, expected yield and locally approved advice. Excessive or poorly timed fertilizer can waste money and harm the environment.

Early weed competition can substantially reduce maize growth. Regular field scouting also helps farmers detect fall armyworm, stem borers, leaf diseases and other problems.

  • Inspect the field regularly, especially during early growth.
  • Control weeds before they compete strongly with maize.
  • Use integrated pest management and identify pests before treatment.
  • Only use locally registered pesticides according to their labels.
  • Keep records of field activities and input costs.

Important for Rwanda-based farmers

Planting dates, suitable varieties, fertilizer rates and pest-control recommendations vary by district, altitude, season and soil condition. Consult local agricultural extension services and Rwanda Agriculture and Animal Resources Development Board (RAB) guidance before making major production decisions.

06 / Harvest & Post-Harvest

Protect the Value of Your Harvest

Harvest Carefully. Store Safely.

Harvest when the crop reaches maturity and conditions allow safe handling. Avoid leaving mature grain exposed unnecessarily to rain, pests and field losses.

After harvest, dry grain on clean surfaces or suitable drying equipment. Grain intended for storage should generally reach around 13–14% moisture or the limit required by the buyer and storage system.

  • Separate visibly moldy or damaged grain.
  • Do not dry maize directly on bare soil.
  • Use clean bags and dry, ventilated storage facilities.
  • Check moisture and inspect stored grain regularly.
  • Prevent aflatoxin risks through good drying and storage practices.
