Pig Farming

A practical guide to raising healthy pigs, improving farm productivity, managing costs, protecting animal health, and building a sustainable pig production system.

01

What Is Pig Farming?

Pig farming is the organized raising and management of pigs for purposes such as meat production, breeding, and income generation. Successful pig farming combines good genetics, proper housing, balanced nutrition, clean water, disease prevention, careful breeding, and accurate farm records.

A productive pig farm is not simply a place where pigs are kept. It is a managed production system where every stage of the animal's life is monitored from birth to market.

Key principle: Healthy pigs + good nutrition + clean housing + strong biosecurity + good records = a more efficient pig farm.
02

Types of Pig Production

Pig farms can be organized according to the production objective and the stage of production.

Breeding Production

Focuses on producing healthy breeding animals, piglets, sows, and boars.

Piglet Production

Focuses on farrowing, early piglet care, survival, and healthy growth.

Grower Production

Raises young pigs efficiently from weaning toward market weight.

Meat Production

Focuses on efficient growth, feed use, animal health, and quality meat production.

03

Common Pig Breeds

Breed selection should consider the local climate, feed availability, disease pressure, production objective, management capacity, and market demand.

Large White

Widely used for commercial production and known for good growth and reproductive performance.

Landrace

Commonly used in breeding programs and known for maternal characteristics and long body structure.

Duroc

Often valued for growth performance and meat characteristics.

Hampshire

Recognized by its characteristic color pattern and used in some meat-production systems.

Crossbreeds

Crossbreeding can combine useful characteristics from different breeds when planned carefully.

Locally Adapted Pigs

Locally adapted animals may provide useful characteristics where environmental conditions and management resources are challenging.

Remember: The most suitable breed is not necessarily the breed with the highest theoretical production. It should be a breed or cross that performs well under your actual farm conditions.
04

Pig Housing and Pen Design

Good housing protects pigs from extreme weather, injuries, contamination, predators, and unnecessary stress while allowing farmers to clean, feed, observe, and manage animals efficiently.

Strong Structure

Buildings should be durable, safe, and easy to maintain.

Ventilation

Good airflow helps remove heat, moisture, dust, and unpleasant gases.

Drainage

Floors and drainage systems should help keep pens reasonably dry and clean.

Easy Access

Farmers should be able to inspect, feed, clean, and move pigs safely.

Important Housing Areas

  • Breeding and boar areas
  • Farrowing areas for sows and piglets
  • Weaner and nursery areas
  • Grower and finisher pens
  • Isolation or quarantine area
  • Feed storage area
  • Equipment cleaning area
  • Manure and waste management area
05

Temperature and Farm Environment

Pigs are sensitive to environmental conditions, especially temperature. Young piglets require warmer conditions than larger pigs, while mature pigs can experience serious heat stress in hot environments.

Young Piglets

Need a warm, dry, draft-free environment with appropriate protection from chilling.

Growing Pigs

Need good ventilation, clean flooring, shade, and protection from excessive heat.

Adult Pigs

Require comfortable housing with adequate airflow, water, space, and shade.

Heat-stress warning: Heavy panting, increased water consumption, lethargy, crowding around water, and reduced feeding can indicate heat stress. Immediate environmental management is important.
06

Feeding and Nutrition

Feed is one of the largest costs in pig production. Efficient feeding requires diets that provide appropriate energy, protein, amino acids, minerals, vitamins, fiber, and other necessary nutrients.

Energy

Supports maintenance, growth, reproduction, and body functions.

Protein & Amino Acids

Important for muscle development, growth, tissue repair, and production.

Vitamins & Minerals

Support bones, immunity, reproduction, metabolism, and general health.

Water

Clean water must be continuously available and accessible.

Feeding According to Production Stage

Stage Main Objective Important Management Focus
Piglets Survival and early development Colostrum, warmth, clean water and suitable starter feed
Weaners Healthy transition and growth Digestible feed, gradual transition and close monitoring
Growers Efficient growth Balanced diet, clean water and feed intake monitoring
Finishers Efficient market-weight production Feed efficiency, health and market planning
Sows Reproduction and milk production Body condition, pregnancy and lactation nutrition
Boars Breeding performance Body condition, fertility and appropriate nutrition
07

Water Management

Water is essential for digestion, temperature regulation, growth, reproduction, and overall health. Water availability should never be treated as an afterthought.

Clean Water

Water should be clean and suitable for animal consumption.

Check Drinkers

Inspect drinkers regularly for blockage, leakage, contamination, and poor flow.

Hot Weather

Monitor water access carefully during periods of high temperature.

08

Piglet Care

The first days and weeks of a piglet's life are extremely important. Good early management improves survival and supports future growth.

1

Colostrum

Make sure newborn piglets receive adequate colostrum as soon as possible after birth.

2

Warmth

Protect piglets from chilling and provide an appropriate warm area.

3

Observation

Check newborn piglets frequently for weakness, injury, starvation, or illness.

4

Hygiene

Keep farrowing areas clean and dry while protecting piglets from contamination.

09

Breeding and Reproduction

Good reproductive management helps maintain a productive herd and reduces unnecessary costs.

  • Select breeding animals based on health, structure, performance, and appropriate breeding records.
  • Monitor females for signs of heat and record breeding dates.
  • Use appropriate breeding or artificial insemination procedures where suitable.
  • Monitor pregnant sows carefully and maintain appropriate nutrition.
  • Prepare clean farrowing facilities before birth.
  • Keep accurate records for each breeding animal.
Breeding goal: Build a healthy, productive herd rather than simply increasing the number of pigs.
10

Pig Health and Disease Prevention

Prevention is a major part of successful pig production. Farmers should monitor animals every day and seek veterinary assistance when disease is suspected.

Daily Observation

Watch appetite, activity, breathing, stool, skin, walking, and general behavior.

Vaccination

Follow a vaccination program appropriate for local disease risks and veterinary advice.

Veterinary Care

Seek professional veterinary guidance when pigs show signs of illness.

Hygiene

Maintain appropriate cleaning, disinfection, and sanitation procedures.

11

Biosecurity

Biosecurity reduces the chance of diseases entering the farm and spreading between animals.

Control Visitors

Limit unnecessary access to animal areas and follow farm hygiene procedures.

Quarantine New Animals

New animals should be managed according to appropriate quarantine and health procedures.

Clean Equipment

Avoid transferring contamination between pens, farms, or animal groups.

Control Rodents and Pests

Rodents, insects, and other pests can contribute to contamination and disease spread.

12

Vaccination and Veterinary Management

Vaccination programs should be designed according to local disease risks, farm conditions, animal age, production system, and veterinary recommendations.

Important: Do not create a vaccination schedule by guessing. Work with a qualified veterinarian or animal-health professional to establish an appropriate program for your farm.
13

Cleaning and Waste Management

Proper waste management protects animal health, farm workers, neighboring communities, and the environment.

Manure Management

Collect and manage manure in a way that reduces contamination and unpleasant odors.

Drainage

Prevent standing wastewater around animal housing.

Composting

Where appropriate, properly managed organic waste can be converted into useful soil amendments.

Environmental Protection

Prevent untreated waste from entering water sources.

14

Growth and Weight Monitoring

Monitoring growth helps farmers identify problems early and understand whether feed, health, genetics, and management are producing the expected results.

Record

Animal identification, dates, weights, treatments, breeding events, and observations.

Compare

Compare growth between groups and production periods.

Investigate

Investigate sudden reductions in appetite, growth, or performance.

15

Meat Production

Efficient meat production depends on healthy animals, appropriate genetics, good nutrition, housing, disease prevention, and careful market planning.

  • Monitor growth and body condition.
  • Minimize unnecessary stress.
  • Maintain clean water availability.
  • Prevent disease and injuries.
  • Keep feed and treatment records.
  • Plan marketing according to local demand.
  • Follow applicable animal welfare and food-safety requirements.
16

Sustainable Pig Farming

Sustainable production aims to use resources efficiently while maintaining animal health, protecting the environment, and supporting the long-term viability of the farm.

Waste Recycling

Explore safe and appropriate ways to manage manure and organic waste.

Water Efficiency

Reduce unnecessary water losses while maintaining adequate animal access.

Resource Efficiency

Improve feed utilization and reduce avoidable production losses.

Smart Energy Use

Where practical, explore efficient energy solutions for farm operations.

17

Farm Records and Management

Good records turn a pig farm from a guessing system into a measurable production business.

Animal Records

Identification, breed, birth date, health events, breeding history, and performance.

Feed Records

Feed purchased, feed used, feed type, wastage, and cost.

Health Records

Treatments, vaccinations, disease observations, deaths, and veterinary visits.

Financial Records

Purchases, labor, veterinary costs, feed costs, sales, and other farm expenses.

18

Daily Pig Farm Checklist

Check every group of pigs for unusual behavior.
Confirm clean and adequate water supply.
Check feed availability and feeding behavior.
Inspect pens, floors, gates, and equipment.
Check ventilation and environmental conditions.
Look for sick, injured, weak, or isolated pigs.
Remove hazards and address hygiene problems.
Record important health, production, and farm events.
19

Warning Signs That Require Attention

Loss of Appetite

A sudden reduction in feed intake may indicate health, environmental, or management problems.

Abnormal Breathing

Coughing, difficult breathing, or unusual respiratory signs should be investigated.

Diarrhea

Persistent or severe diarrhea requires prompt attention.

Sudden Deaths

Unexpected deaths should be treated seriously and investigated with professional guidance.

Severe Weakness

Pigs that become unusually weak or isolated require assessment.

Rapid Disease Spread

Multiple animals showing similar signs require immediate attention and appropriate biosecurity.

20

Pig Farm Planning

Before expanding a pig farm, farmers should consider production goals, land, housing, feed supply, water availability, veterinary support, labor, capital, market access, and environmental management.

01

Define Your Goal

Decide whether the farm focuses on breeding, piglets, growers, finishers, or an integrated production system.

02

Plan Resources

Calculate housing, feed, water, labor, veterinary, and equipment requirements.

03

Know the Market

Understand local buyers, demand, timing, transport, and expected production costs.

04

Measure Performance

Use records to identify what is working and where the farm needs improvement.

21

Golden Rules of Pig Farming

01 Provide clean and safe water every day.
02 Feed pigs according to their production stage.
03 Keep housing clean, dry, safe, and well ventilated.
04 Monitor animals every day.
05 Protect the farm through strong biosecurity.
06 Keep accurate production and financial records.
07 Seek veterinary advice when disease is suspected.
08 Manage manure and farm waste responsibly.
09 Select breeding animals carefully.
10 Make decisions using farm records rather than guesses.

Healthy Pigs. Better Management. Stronger Farms.

Successful pig farming is built through consistent care, good nutrition, strong biosecurity, responsible breeding, accurate records, and continuous learning.
