01

Understanding Chicken Farming

Chicken farming is the practice of raising chickens for meat, eggs, breeding stock, and other useful farm products. It is one of the most widely practiced poultry enterprises because chickens can be managed in different production systems and adapted to many farming environments.

Successful chicken production depends on several connected areas: good genetics, quality feed, clean water, suitable housing, proper temperature and ventilation, disease prevention, careful observation, and accurate farm records.

Meat Production

Raising chickens specifically for efficient growth and quality meat production.

Egg Production

Managing laying hens to produce clean, safe, and consistent eggs.

Breeding

Producing fertile eggs, chicks, and replacement breeding stock.

Farm Performance

Using records to monitor growth, production, feed use, health, and farm efficiency.

02

Major Types of Chicken Production

The right production type depends on the farmer's goals, available resources, market demand, housing system, and management capacity.

Broilers

Chickens selected and managed primarily for meat production. The focus is on healthy growth, efficient feed use, good housing, and consistent management.

Layers

Chickens managed mainly for egg production. Good pullet development, nutrition, lighting, health, nesting, and egg handling are essential.

Breeders

Breeding chickens are managed to produce fertile eggs and healthy offspring. Breeding flock health and reproductive performance are especially important.

Dual-Purpose Chickens

Some breeds and local chickens can provide both eggs and meat. They can be useful where farmers prefer flexibility rather than highly specialized production.

04

Chicken Production Systems

SYSTEM 01

Intensive System

Chickens are kept in controlled housing where the farmer manages feeding, watering, health, lighting, ventilation, and production conditions.

  • Controlled environment
  • Efficient use of space
  • Close health monitoring
  • Requires good management
SYSTEM 02

Semi-Intensive System

Chickens have housing but may also receive access to an outdoor area depending on the management system and local conditions.

  • Combination of housing and outdoor access
  • Flexible management
  • Requires predator protection
  • Good hygiene remains important
SYSTEM 03

Free-Range System

Chickens have access to outdoor areas where they can move and forage while still receiving planned feed, water, shelter, and health management.

  • Outdoor movement
  • Foraging opportunity
  • Requires secure range areas
  • Weather and predator risks must be managed

06

Broiler Production

Broiler production focuses on raising healthy chickens efficiently for meat. Management decisions made during the first days of life can strongly influence later growth and flock performance.

Prepare the House

Clean, disinfect, dry, and prepare the poultry house before chicks arrive.

Manage Temperature

Provide suitable warmth during brooding and monitor chick behavior rather than relying only on a single temperature reading.

Provide Feed

Supply a nutritionally appropriate ration for the bird's age and production stage.

Protect Water Supply

Ensure birds have continuous access to clean, fresh water.

Ventilation

Maintain fresh air while avoiding harmful drafts, excessive dust, moisture, and ammonia buildup.

Monitor Growth

Regularly monitor body weight, feed use, mortality, flock uniformity, and general bird condition.

07

Layer Production

Layer production requires careful management before and during the laying period. Strong pullet development, balanced nutrition, good housing, appropriate lighting, clean water, and disease prevention all contribute to productive flocks.

01

Pullet Development

Young birds should develop strong body condition, skeletal structure, and healthy feathering before entering the laying stage.

02

Nutrition

Laying birds require diets that support egg production, body maintenance, and eggshell quality.

03

Lighting

A consistent lighting program can help manage the laying cycle. Changes should be planned carefully.

04

Nesting

Clean and accessible nests encourage proper laying behavior and help reduce dirty or damaged eggs.

05

Egg Collection

Regular collection reduces breakage, contamination, and unnecessary egg losses.

06

Production Records

Daily egg numbers, damaged eggs, mortality, feed use, and flock condition should be recorded.

08

Chicken Housing

Good housing protects chickens from harsh weather, predators, disease exposure, overcrowding, and poor environmental conditions.

Dry and clean floor
Good ventilation
Protection from predators
Enough feeding space
Easy access to clean water
Suitable lighting
Clean nesting areas
Good drainage around buildings

Litter Management

Poultry litter should remain reasonably dry and loose. Wet litter can increase odor, ammonia, foot problems, bacterial growth, and other health risks. Leaking drinkers, poor ventilation, excessive stocking density, and water entering the house should be corrected.

09

Brooding Chicks

The brooding period is one of the most sensitive stages of chicken production. Chicks need suitable warmth, protection from drafts, easy access to feed and water, clean litter, and close observation.

Warmth

Provide appropriate heat and adjust conditions according to chick behavior and environmental conditions.

Draft Protection

Protect young chicks from strong cold drafts while still providing adequate fresh air.

Early Feeding

Make suitable chick feed easily accessible so young birds can begin feeding normally.

Water Access

Ensure clean water is available and that chicks can easily reach drinkers.

Watch Chick Behavior

Chick behavior provides useful clues. Excessive huddling may indicate cold or drafts, while birds moving far away from the heat source may indicate excessive heat.

Grower Feed Steady body development Balanced growth and feed intake

Breeder Feed Reproduction and fertility Balanced nutrients and controlled body condition

Energy

Supports body maintenance, movement, growth, reproduction, and production.

Protein & Amino Acids

Support muscle development, feather growth, tissue repair, and egg production.

Minerals

Calcium and phosphorus are particularly important for skeletal development and eggshell formation.

Vitamins

Support metabolism, immunity, growth, reproduction, and general health.

11

Water Management

Water is one of the most important resources in poultry production. Birds should have reliable access to clean, fresh water appropriate for their age and production system.

Clean Water

Keep water clean and protect it from contamination by litter, manure, feed, and other sources.

Check Drinkers

Inspect drinkers regularly for leaks, blockages, poor positioning, or insufficient water flow.

Heat Management

During hot conditions, pay close attention to water availability because water intake can increase.

Hygiene

Clean and maintain water equipment as part of the farm's routine hygiene program.

12

Lighting Management

Lighting influences chicken activity, feeding behavior, growth management, reproductive development, and laying performance. A lighting program should be appropriate to the bird's age, production type, housing system, and management objectives.

01

Consistency

Maintain a consistent lighting routine rather than making unnecessary sudden changes.

02

Uniform Coverage

Avoid areas that are excessively dark or excessively bright within the poultry house.

03

Production Stage

Lighting requirements change as chickens move from brooding and growing to laying or breeding.

04

Energy Efficiency

Efficient lighting equipment can reduce electricity use while providing suitable illumination.

13

Breeding & Hatching

Successful chick production begins with healthy breeding birds and proper management of fertile eggs.

Breeder Selection

Select healthy birds with suitable characteristics and maintain good body condition.

Egg Collection

Collect hatching eggs carefully and avoid unnecessary cracking, contamination, and rough handling.

Egg Storage

Store hatching eggs under suitable conditions and avoid unnecessarily long storage periods.

Incubation

Maintain appropriate incubation temperature, humidity, turning, ventilation, and hygiene.

Hatching

Handle newly hatched chicks carefully and move them into a properly prepared brooding environment.

Feed Conversion & Growth Performance

Feed conversion helps farmers understand how efficiently feed is being converted into body weight. It is especially useful in meat production.

FCR FORMULA
Feed Consumed ÷ Weight Gain

Lower feed conversion values generally indicate more efficient feed use when comparing birds under similar production conditions.

Feed Quality

Nutrient balance and feed quality influence growth and feed efficiency.

Water Availability

Poor access to water can reduce feed intake and negatively affect performance.

Environment

Heat, cold, ventilation, litter condition, and housing quality can affect growth.

Health

Disease and parasites can reduce growth and increase feed costs.

16

Common Chicken Diseases & Health Conditions

Disease recognition and prevention are essential parts of poultry management. Symptoms can overlap between different diseases, so suspected outbreaks should be assessed by a qualified veterinary professional.

Newcastle Disease

A serious viral disease that can cause respiratory, nervous, digestive, and mortality problems.

Avian Influenza

A viral disease of birds that can cause severe illness and requires strict biosecurity and veterinary or official attention when suspected.

Infectious Bronchitis

A contagious viral disease primarily affecting the respiratory system and potentially egg production.

Infectious Bursal Disease

Also called Gumboro disease, it can affect young chickens and compromise immune function.

Coccidiosis

A parasitic disease associated with intestinal damage, especially where litter management is poor.

Fowl Pox

A viral disease that may cause lesions on the skin and can also affect the mouth or respiratory tract.

Marek's Disease

A viral disease that can affect nerves, organs, and immune function in chickens.

Fowl Cholera

A bacterial disease that can cause illness and mortality, particularly in poorly protected flocks.

Salmonellosis

Bacterial infections can affect bird health and are also important from a food-safety perspective.

Important Veterinary Note

Do not rely on symptoms alone to diagnose a poultry disease. When unusual sickness, sudden deaths, respiratory signs, diarrhea, neurological signs, or major drops in production occur, contact a qualified veterinarian or appropriate animal-health authority.

17

Disease Prevention & Biosecurity

Prevention is usually safer and less costly than managing a serious disease outbreak. Biosecurity reduces the opportunity for pathogens to enter or spread within a flock.

01

Control Visitors

Limit unnecessary access to poultry houses and maintain clear visitor procedures.

02

Clean Equipment

Clean and disinfect equipment before moving it between poultry groups whenever practical.

03

Isolate Sick Birds

Separate visibly sick birds when appropriate and seek veterinary guidance.

04

Protect Feed & Water

Prevent contamination of feed and water by wild birds, rodents, manure, and other sources.

05

Manage Rodents

Keep poultry areas clean and use an appropriate rodent-control program.

06

Dispose of Carcasses Safely

Handle dead birds promptly and dispose of them according to local veterinary and environmental requirements.

18

Vaccination & Veterinary Management

Vaccination programs should be designed according to the production system, local disease risks, bird age, previous disease history, and veterinary recommendations.

Plan Vaccination

Follow a locally appropriate vaccination program prepared with qualified animal-health professionals.

Keep Records

Record vaccine names, dates, flock information, and other relevant treatment details.

Investigate Problems

Unusual mortality or production changes should be investigated instead of simply treating symptoms.

Observe Withdrawal Periods

Follow veterinary and product instructions for medicines and food-producing animals.

19

Stocking Density

Stocking density should not be treated as one fixed number for every poultry house. Suitable stocking levels depend on bird size, age, housing design, ventilation, climate, litter quality, equipment, and management.

Available Space

Birds need enough space to move, feed, drink, rest, and express normal behavior.

Ventilation Capacity

Housing must be able to maintain acceptable air quality at the planned flock size.

Climate

Hot and humid environments may require different management approaches from cooler conditions.

Bird Welfare

Overcrowding can increase competition, stress, litter problems, injury risk, and disease pressure.

20

Heat & Cold Stress

Heat Stress

  • Provide adequate clean water
  • Improve air movement
  • Reduce unnecessary handling
  • Monitor bird behavior
  • Maintain suitable stocking levels
  • Protect houses from excessive solar heat

Cold Stress

  • Provide appropriate brooding warmth
  • Protect young chicks from drafts
  • Keep litter dry
  • Monitor bird behavior
  • Prevent unnecessary heat loss
  • Maintain fresh air without harmful drafts
21

Chicken Welfare

Good welfare supports healthy birds and responsible production. Farmers should observe chickens regularly and respond quickly when birds show signs of distress, injury, disease, hunger, thirst, or environmental discomfort.

Freedom from Thirst

Provide reliable access to clean water.

Good Nutrition

Provide appropriate feed for each production stage.

Suitable Environment

Maintain clean, safe, comfortable housing.

Health Protection

Prevent disease and respond to health problems early.

Normal Behavior

Provide appropriate space and suitable management.

22

Record Keeping & Farm Performance

Good records turn daily farm activities into useful information. They help farmers identify problems, measure progress, calculate costs, and make better management decisions.

Daily Records

Feed use, water observations, mortality, egg numbers, environmental observations, and unusual events.

Growth Records

Monitor body weight and flock uniformity where appropriate.

Egg Records

Record total eggs, cracked eggs, dirty eggs, rejected eggs, and other quality information.

Health Records

Keep vaccination, treatment, disease, mortality, and veterinary records.

Financial Records

Track feed, chicks, medication, labor, equipment, transport, sales, and other farm costs.

Advantages & Challenges

Potential Advantages

  • Egg and meat markets

  • Can be integrated with other farming activities

Modern Farming Principle

Good poultry farming is not only about keeping birds alive. It is about creating a system where genetics, nutrition, water, housing, health, welfare, records, technology, and market planning work together.

27

Chicken Farming Quick Summary

Successful chicken farming is built on consistent daily management. Start with healthy birds, provide appropriate feed and clean water, maintain suitable housing and ventilation, prevent disease through biosecurity, monitor flock performance, keep accurate records, and respond quickly to problems.
