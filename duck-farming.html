01 / FOUNDATION

Understanding Duck Farming

Duck farming is the raising and management of ducks for meat, eggs, breeding stock, feathers, and other agricultural purposes. Ducks can adapt to different production systems when they receive suitable housing, nutrition, clean water, disease protection, and proper management.

Ducks are valuable poultry because many breeds can perform well under both intensive and semi-intensive systems. Successful production depends on choosing suitable breeds, providing balanced feed, maintaining good hygiene, and monitoring the birds throughout their production cycle.

02 / BREEDS

Types of Ducks

Different duck breeds have different characteristics. Farmers should select breeds according to the main production goal, climate, available resources, and market demand.

Pekin Duck

A widely used meat duck known for rapid growth, good feed utilization, and strong meat-production potential.

Khaki Campbell

A productive laying breed valued for its ability to produce a large number of eggs under suitable management.

Indian Runner

An active breed traditionally known for egg production, upright body carriage, and strong foraging ability.

Muscovy Duck

A hardy duck type often raised for meat and well suited to a variety of smallholder and semi-intensive systems.

Dual-Purpose Ducks

Selected for a combination of useful meat and egg production, allowing farmers to diversify output.

Improved Breeds

Commercial and improved strains may be selected for specific traits such as growth, egg production, efficiency, or adaptation.

03 / FARM SYSTEMS

Duck Production Systems

Intensive System

Ducks are kept under controlled housing with managed feeding, watering, ventilation, health care, and production monitoring.

  • Controlled environment
  • Easy record keeping
  • Efficient feed management
  • Higher management requirements

Semi-Intensive System

Ducks receive managed housing and supplementary feed while also having controlled access to outdoor areas.

  • Flexible management
  • Outdoor access
  • Supplementary feeding
  • Moderate investment

Extensive System

Ducks have greater access to natural environments and forage, while the farmer provides additional feed, shelter, and protection.

  • Lower infrastructure needs
  • Natural foraging
  • More environmental exposure
  • Requires strong predator control

Duck Production Cycle

Understanding each stage helps farmers provide the right management at the right time.

05 / HOUSING

Duck Housing

Good housing protects ducks from extreme weather, predators, injuries, disease exposure, and poor environmental conditions. The house should be clean, dry, well ventilated, secure, and large enough for the number of birds kept.

Dry and clean flooring
Good ventilation
Protection from predators
Adequate space
Easy access to feed and water
Suitable nesting areas
Proper drainage
Easy cleaning and disinfection

Litter Management

Wet litter can encourage harmful microorganisms, ammonia buildup, foot problems, and poor air quality. Remove wet bedding, repair leaking drinkers, improve drainage, and maintain appropriate ventilation.

06 / WATER

Water Management

Water is one of the most important resources in duck production. Ducks need reliable access to clean drinking water, and their water systems should be designed to reduce contamination and excessive wetness.

Clean Drinking Water

Provide clean water regularly and monitor drinkers throughout the day.

Water Quality

Water should be safe, clean, and protected from contamination by manure, feed, chemicals, and waste.

Wading Areas

Where water access is provided for management or welfare, keep the area clean and prevent standing contaminated water.

Regular Cleaning

Clean and inspect drinkers and water facilities regularly to maintain hygiene.

Important

Ducks naturally interact heavily with water, but excessive wetness around housing can damage litter quality and increase hygiene problems. Good drainage and water-system management are essential.

07 / NUTRITION

Feeding & Nutrition

Ducks require balanced diets containing energy, protein, amino acids, vitamins, minerals, and sufficient water. Nutritional requirements change as birds move from early growth to adult production.

Stage
Management Focus
Duckling Starter
High-quality starter nutrition to support early growth, feather development, and healthy body development.
Grower
Balanced energy, protein, amino acids, vitamins, and minerals for efficient growth.
Finisher
Nutrition designed to support efficient finishing and desired market weight in meat ducks.
Layer
Appropriate energy, protein, amino acids, calcium, phosphorus, and vitamins to support egg production and shell quality.
Breeder
Balanced breeder nutrition supporting body condition, fertility, egg production, and hatchability.
Energy Protein Amino Acids Calcium Phosphorus Vitamins Minerals Clean Water

Feed Management

Feed should be stored in a dry, clean, pest-resistant place. Avoid moldy or spoiled feed and monitor feed intake so that unusual changes can be detected early.

08 / EFFICIENCY

Feed Conversion & Growth Efficiency

Feed conversion is an important measure of production efficiency, especially in meat duck farming. Farmers can use records to monitor how much feed is required to produce a given amount of live weight gain.

FCR = Feed Consumed ÷ Live Weight Gain

Lower feed conversion values generally indicate more efficient conversion of feed into body weight under comparable production conditions.

Genetics

Breed and genetic potential influence growth rate and production efficiency.

Nutrition

Balanced feed supports healthy growth and efficient nutrient use.

Environment

Temperature, ventilation, space, and litter quality influence performance.

Health

Disease and poor health can reduce feed intake and production efficiency.

09 / REPRODUCTION

Breeding & Incubation

Successful duck breeding requires healthy parent stock, suitable mating management, proper nutrition, careful egg collection, appropriate egg storage, and good incubation practices.

Breeding Management

  • Select healthy and productive breeding stock.
  • Maintain suitable body condition.
  • Provide balanced breeder nutrition.
  • Monitor fertility and hatchability.
  • Reduce unnecessary stress.
  • Keep accurate breeding records.

Egg Handling

  • Collect eggs regularly.
  • Handle eggs carefully.
  • Separate damaged or unsuitable eggs.
  • Store hatching eggs correctly.
  • Maintain good hygiene.
  • Use appropriate incubation conditions.
10 / EARLY LIFE

Brooding Ducklings

Ducklings are especially sensitive during their early life. Proper brooding helps them maintain body temperature, find feed and water, and develop strong immunity and healthy growth.

Temperature

Provide suitable warmth and adjust the brooding environment gradually as ducklings grow and develop better temperature control.

Feed & Water

Ducklings should have easy access to appropriate starter feed and clean drinking water.

Protection

Protect ducklings from chilling, predators, wet litter, overcrowding, injuries, and contaminated environments.

Water Safety for Ducklings

Young ducklings can become chilled or exhausted in unsuitable water environments. Any water access should be managed carefully, with safe access and easy exit points where appropriate.

11 / HEALTH

Common Duck Diseases

Disease problems can cause poor growth, reduced egg production, mortality, and economic losses. Early observation and veterinary support are important when disease is suspected.

VIRAL

Duck Viral Enteritis

A serious viral disease that can affect ducks and may cause weakness, reduced production, and mortality.

VIRAL

Duck Viral Hepatitis

A disease mainly affecting young ducklings and capable of causing severe losses in susceptible populations.

VIRAL

Avian Influenza

A potentially serious infectious disease requiring strong biosecurity and immediate professional attention when suspected.

VIRAL / BACTERIAL

Newcastle Disease

An infectious poultry disease that can cause respiratory, nervous, digestive, or production problems depending on the strain and affected birds.

BACTERIAL

Riemerella Infection

A bacterial infection that may cause respiratory, neurological, and systemic disease in ducks.

PARASITIC

Coccidiosis

A parasitic disease that can damage the intestinal tract, particularly when birds are exposed to contaminated environments.

FUNGAL

Aspergillosis

A fungal respiratory disease associated with exposure to contaminated or moldy materials.

TOXIN

Botulism

A condition caused by toxins produced by certain bacteria, often associated with contaminated environments or decaying organic material.

MANAGEMENT

Wet Litter Problems

Poor litter management can increase exposure to harmful organisms and contribute to foot, skin, respiratory, and hygiene problems.

Veterinary Guidance

If unusual mortality, severe illness, sudden drops in production, breathing problems, neurological signs, or other serious symptoms occur, seek advice from a qualified veterinarian or animal-health professional. Treatment and vaccination decisions should be based on local veterinary guidance and applicable regulations.

13 / SPACE

Stocking Density

Ducks need enough space to move, rest, feed, drink, and behave normally. Stocking density should take into account bird age, breed, production system, housing design, ventilation, climate, litter condition, and access to outdoor areas.

Age

Older and larger birds require more space than young ducklings.

Breed

Body size and production type influence appropriate housing space.

Climate

Hot conditions require greater attention to space and ventilation.

Management

Good litter, water, feeding, and ventilation systems help maintain a healthy environment.

14 / CLIMATE

Heat & Cold Stress

Heat Stress

  • Rapid breathing or panting
  • Reduced feed intake
  • Increased water consumption
  • Reduced activity
  • Lower production

Improve ventilation, provide cool clean water, reduce unnecessary handling, and provide shade or other appropriate cooling measures.

Cold Stress

  • Huddling together
  • Reduced activity
  • Increased feed demand
  • Chilling in young birds
  • Poor growth

Protect young ducks from cold conditions while maintaining sufficient ventilation and avoiding excessive humidity.

15 / WELFARE

Duck Welfare

Good welfare means providing ducks with conditions that support health, comfort, safety, and normal behaviour.

Good Nutrition

Provide appropriate feed and reliable access to clean drinking water.

Comfortable Housing

Maintain clean, dry, secure, and well-ventilated housing.

Protection

Protect birds from predators, injury, disease, and unnecessary stress.

Natural Behaviour

Where the production system allows, provide suitable opportunities for normal duck behaviours.

19 / FARMING REALITY

Advantages & Challenges

Advantages of Duck Farming

  • Can provide both meat and eggs.
  • Several breeds are available for different goals.
  • Can be integrated into diversified farms.
  • Some breeds have strong foraging ability.
  • Duck manure can contribute to farm nutrient cycling.
  • Can be suitable for smallholder and commercial systems.

Challenges

  • Water management can create wet housing conditions.
  • Feed costs can significantly affect profitability.
  • Disease outbreaks can cause serious losses.
  • Predators can threaten free-range birds.
  • Overcrowding can reduce welfare and performance.
  • Good biosecurity requires consistent management.
20 / DAILY MANAGEMENT

Important Duck Management Points

01 Provide clean drinking water every day.
02 Provide balanced feed appropriate for the production stage.
03 Keep housing clean, dry, secure, and well ventilated.
04 Monitor birds for changes in behaviour or health.
05 Control contact with wild birds and other disease sources.
06 Prevent overcrowding.
07 Collect and handle eggs carefully.
08 Keep accurate production records.
09 Respond quickly to unusual illness or mortality.
10 Seek veterinary advice when serious disease is suspected.
Good management principle: Healthy ducks + good nutrition + clean water + suitable housing + strong biosecurity + careful records = better production.
