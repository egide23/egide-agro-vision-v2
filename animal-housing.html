Why Animal Housing Matters

Good housing is not simply a building. It is part of the production system that protects animals, controls disease, reduces feed wastage, improves comfort and makes daily farm management easier.

A good livestock house should provide protection from rain, excessive heat, cold winds, predators and theft while maintaining ventilation, drainage, cleanliness and sufficient space. In Rwanda, housing should be designed around the local climate, available materials, farm size, animal type and business objective.

Business principle: do not spend all your starting capital on an expensive building. A well-designed, durable and easy-to-clean agricultural structure can often produce better value than an attractive building with unnecessary finishes.

10 Principles of Good Animal Housing

These principles should be considered before purchasing materials.

1

Good Site

Choose raised, well-drained ground away from flooding and stagnant water.

2

Ventilation

Fresh air must enter and hot, humid air must escape without creating harmful drafts.

3

Drainage

Rainwater, washing water and manure should not remain inside the building.

4

Correct Space

Avoid overcrowding. Calculate floor area according to animal number, size, age and production system.

5

Easy Cleaning

Floors, walls, feeders and drinkers should be designed for regular cleaning and disinfection.

6

Feed Access

Feeding areas should allow all animals to reach feed without unnecessary competition.

7

Water Access

Clean drinking water should be continuously available and protected from contamination.

8

Biosecurity

Control visitors, rodents, wild birds, dirty footwear and equipment that can introduce disease.

9

Animal Welfare

Housing should allow animals to rest, feed, drink and move normally without unnecessary injury.

10

Future Expansion

Leave enough space around the building for future pens, feed storage, manure management and expansion.

How to Calculate Housing Size

Never choose a building size only because it looks large enough. Calculate the usable animal area first.

Basic Formula

Required animal area = Number of animals × recommended floor space per animal

Example: if 100 adult chickens require approximately 0.27 m² each:

100 × 0.27 m² = 27 m² minimum animal floor area

A practical design can then provide additional working margin, equipment space and easier movement. Therefore, a 30 m² house such as 6 m × 5 m can be used for the planning example below.

1. Housing for 100 Chickens

A practical starter model for a small commercial chicken enterprise.

Recommended Structure

  • House: 6 m × 5 m
  • Floor area: 30 m²
  • Raised above surrounding ground
  • Concrete floor with drainage
  • Good cross ventilation
  • Wide roof overhang
  • Separate feed storage area
  • Footbath at entrance

Space Calculation

100 chickens × 0.27 m² = 27 m² minimum

A 30 m² building provides a practical margin for movement, equipment and management.

For layers, provide adequate nest and perch facilities according to the production system.

Estimated Investment

RWF 4.5M – 7.0M

Planning range for a durable small commercial structure, depending on materials, labor, location and equipment.

2. Housing for 50 Turkeys

Turkeys require substantially more space as they mature.

Recommended Structure

  • House: 7 m × 6 m
  • Indoor area: 42 m²
  • Large ventilation openings
  • Dry deep-litter floor
  • Protected feeding area
  • Secure outside run
  • Predator protection

Space Calculation

50 × 0.7–0.9 m² = 35–45 m²

A 42 m² house fits within this planning range for mature breeding-sized birds.

Estimated Investment

RWF 6.0M – 9.0M

Excludes birds, feed and major land-development costs.

Important: Turkeys should not be overcrowded. They can become stressed and may injure one another during frightening events or poor management.

3. Housing for 50 Ducks

Ducks need a dry sleeping area plus access to a suitable outdoor exercise area.

House Design

  • House: 6 m × 5 m
  • Indoor floor: 30 m²
  • Dry bedding
  • Strong ventilation
  • Non-slip floor
  • Outdoor fenced run
  • Drainage around water areas

Outdoor Area

A useful business design should provide an additional fenced outdoor area rather than trying to put all activities inside the sleeping house.

Example planning area: 50 m² or more outdoor run

Estimated Investment

RWF 4.5M – 7.0M

Water facilities and extensive pond construction can increase the investment considerably.

4. Housing for 10 Adult Pigs

Pig housing must prioritize drainage, hygiene, ventilation and manure management.

Recommended Structure

  • Overall building: about 30 m²
  • Separate pens where appropriate
  • Concrete non-slip floor
  • Drainage channel
  • Manure collection area
  • Feed storage nearby
  • Good roof overhang
  • Open-sided ventilation

Why Drainage Matters

Pig houses should not allow water and manure to remain around resting areas. FAO guidance recommends concrete floors for confined systems and appropriate floor slopes toward manure drainage areas.

5. Housing for 10 Adult Goats

Goats need a dry, well-ventilated shelter protected from excessive rain and muddy conditions.

Recommended Structure

  • House: 5 m × 5 m
  • Total floor area: 25 m²
  • Raised or well-drained floor
  • Feed trough
  • Clean water point
  • Separate kidding area
  • Outside exercise yard

Space Calculation

10 adult goats × approximately 1.1 m² = 11 m² animal resting area

The 25 m² structure provides room for feeding, handling, temporary separation, kidding and management activities.

Estimated Investment

RWF 4.0M – 6.5M

A simpler locally sourced structure may cost less; a raised timber/slatted system can increase the initial investment.

6. Housing for 10 Adult Sheep

Sheep housing should remain dry, clean and well ventilated.

Recommended Structure

  • House: 5 m × 5 m
  • Total floor area: 25 m²
  • Dry resting area
  • Good roof overhang
  • Feed trough
  • Water point
  • Small lambing/separation pen
  • Outdoor yard

Space Calculation

10 adult sheep × approximately 1.1 m² = 11 m² resting requirement

Additional floor area is useful for handling, feeding, separation and future expansion.

Estimated Investment

RWF 4.0M – 6.5M

Local materials and an open-sided design can reduce construction cost while maintaining functionality.

7. One Farm House for 5 Cattle + 10 Goats + 10 Sheep

A mixed livestock farm should not simply put every animal into one undivided room. The building can share the same roof while maintaining separate animal zones.

Planning Calculation

5 cattle × approximately 5 m² = 25 m²
10 goats × approximately 1.1 m² = 11 m²
10 sheep × approximately 1.1 m² = 11 m²
Basic animal area ≈ 47 m²

After adding feed alleys, partitions, handling space, water points, storage and working space, a practical covered building of around 80 m² is a useful starter design.

What Your Housing Budget Should Cover

1. Site Preparation

Clearing, leveling, drainage and preparation of the building foundation.

2. Foundation

Foundation suitable for the selected wall and roof structure.

3. Floor

Durable, washable and appropriately sloped floors where concrete is required.

4. Walls

Brick, block, timber, poles, mesh or combinations according to animal and climate requirements.

5. Roofing

Durable roofing with adequate overhang and ventilation.

6. Drainage

Channels, collection points and safe disposal or reuse of manure and wastewater.

7. Feeding Equipment

Feed troughs, feeders, storage and access points.

8. Water Equipment

Drinkers, pipes, tanks and reliable clean-water access.

9. Biosecurity

Footbath, controlled entrance, cleaning equipment and isolation space.

How to Start an Animal-Housing Business in Rwanda

The building is only one part of the investment. Plan the whole production system before construction begins.

1

Choose the Enterprise

Decide whether the business will focus on eggs, meat, breeding, milk, breeding stock or multiple livestock enterprises.

2

Study Your Market

Identify nearby buyers, restaurants, hotels, processors, traders, households and livestock markets.

3

Calculate Feed

Feed can become one of the largest operating costs. Calculate expected consumption before buying animals.

4

Plan Water

Confirm that clean water will be available every day before stocking animals.

5

Build for Expansion

Design the first structure so that another house can be added without rebuilding the whole farm.

6

Keep Records

Record construction costs, animals, feed, medicines, mortality, production, sales and profit.

Simple Farm Investment Formula

Total Starting Capital

Housing + Animals + Feed Reserve + Equipment + Water + Biosecurity + Emergency Reserve = Starting Capital

A common mistake is to spend nearly all available money on the building and then have insufficient capital to feed the animals. A good business plan protects operating cash after construction.

Example planning rule: Keep a working-capital reserve for feed, health and unexpected expenses after construction.

Before You Build: 15-Point Checklist

  • Confirm the land is suitable and well drained.
  • Check access for vehicles and construction materials.
  • Identify the prevailing wind and sunlight pattern.
  • Calculate the required animal floor area.
  • Plan feeding and watering positions.
  • Plan drainage before pouring the floor.
  • Plan manure collection and storage.
  • Provide adequate ventilation.
  • Plan a biosecurity entrance.
  • Include an isolation or treatment area where appropriate.
  • Protect feed from moisture, rodents and contamination.
  • Provide reliable water.
  • Compare at least several local contractor quotations.
  • Keep a contingency amount for price changes and unforeseen work.
  • Plan how the farm will expand after the first production cycle.

Final Advice for Farmers

The best animal house is not necessarily the most expensive one. It is the one that provides the correct space, good ventilation, drainage, cleanliness, animal protection and efficient daily management at a cost the farm can realistically support.

For a new farmer in Rwanda, it is usually safer to start with a manageable number of animals, build a structure that can expand, maintain sufficient working capital and grow according to actual market demand.

Rwanda's agricultural development programs also emphasize climate-smart livestock production, improved productivity, nutrition, manure management and integration between crops and livestock.

Build Better. Manage Better. Farm Better.

Good housing creates the foundation for healthy animals, efficient management and sustainable livestock production.
