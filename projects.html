FARM INFRASTRUCTURE

The Pig House Should Be Simple, Clean & Efficient

The objective is not an expensive building. The objective is a structure that protects the animals, makes cleaning easier and allows efficient management.

HOUSING PLAN

Design the House Around the Pigs

A starter unit for approximately 12 growing pigs should prioritize ventilation, drainage, shade, easy feeding and easy cleaning.

Practical pen space Allow enough space for pigs to move, rest and feed without excessive crowding.
Strong ventilation Keep the house naturally ventilated while protecting pigs from excessive drafts.
Reliable water Drinking water should be available consistently and kept clean.
Good drainage Floors should allow waste water to leave the building instead of remaining around pigs.
Easy cleaning Layout should allow workers to clean pens quickly every day.

FEEDING & NUTRITION

Feed Management Controls the Business

Feed is one of the largest recurring expenses in pig farming. Reducing waste while maintaining adequate nutrition is therefore central to profitability.

Starter Stage

Newly purchased pigs should receive an appropriate transition and starter ration. Avoid sudden changes in feed whenever possible.

Grower Stage

As pigs gain weight, feeding should support steady growth while avoiding unnecessary feed waste.

Finisher Stage

The final stage focuses on reaching the intended market weight efficiently and maintaining good body condition.

Reduce Waste

Feeders should be checked daily. Spilled or contaminated feed represents money that leaves the business without producing additional weight.

Water

Clean water is essential. Water availability should be checked every day, especially during hot weather.

Professional Advice

Feed formulations and rations should be adapted to the pigs' age, genetics, health status and available feed ingredients.

PRODUCTION ROADMAP

From Purchase To Market

A simplified six-month planning cycle. Actual growth speed depends on breed, starting weight, nutrition, health and management.

1

Month 1 — Purchase & Adaptation

Purchase healthy pigs, prepare housing, allow animals to adapt, establish feeding, begin records and monitor health closely.

2

Month 2 — Stable Growth

Maintain consistent feeding, clean housing, adequate water and daily observation.

3

Month 3 — Grower Stage

Monitor body weight, feed consumption and differences in growth between pigs.

4

Month 4 — Production Monitoring

Review feed efficiency, health records, housing conditions and expected market timing.

5

Month 5 — Finishing

Continue the finishing program and begin contacting potential buyers instead of waiting until the last moment.

6

Month 6 — Market & Evaluate

Sell animals at the planned market weight, calculate the actual result and use the records to improve the next production cycle.

ANIMAL HEALTH

Healthy Pigs Protect the Investment

Disease prevention is generally more effective than trying to recover animals after serious illness.

Daily Observation

Check appetite, movement, breathing, stool, body condition and unusual behaviour.

Veterinary Plan

Work with a qualified animal-health professional to establish appropriate preventive treatment.

Hygiene

Keep pens, feeders, drinkers and surrounding areas clean and dry as practical.

Early Isolation

Suspected sick animals should be assessed promptly and separated when appropriate.

📈 Future Cycles

The major construction investment does not normally need to be repeated every cycle.

The main recurring expenses become pigs, feed, health, water, cleaning and other operating costs.

Better records and management can also help the farmer identify where costs can be reduced without damaging animal performance.

FARM MANAGEMENT

The Farmer's Daily System

A profitable pig farm is managed every day. These simple habits help protect the investment.

Check every pig every day

Look for changes in appetite, movement, breathing and general behaviour.

Record feed use

Track how much feed is purchased and used. Feed is one of the most important costs.

Record animal weights

Regular weight checks help measure growth and improve decisions about market timing.

Keep health records

Record treatments, illnesses, deaths and veterinary recommendations.

Clean consistently

Good hygiene should be part of the daily routine rather than something done occasionally.

Start marketing early

Identify possible buyers before pigs reach the target market weight.

Protect the feed store

Keep feed dry and protected from rodents, contamination and unnecessary losses.

Calculate after every cycle

Compare actual costs and revenue with the original plan before starting the next cycle.
