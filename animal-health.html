What Is Animal Health?

Animal health is the overall physical condition, nutrition, welfare and disease resistance of livestock and poultry. Healthy animals generally grow better, reproduce more efficiently and use feed more effectively.

Good animal health is not only about treating sick animals. Prevention is usually the foundation of a successful livestock enterprise.

Farmer Principle

Prevention, early observation, good hygiene, proper nutrition and professional veterinary advice can greatly reduce avoidable animal-health problems.

Five Foundations of Animal Health

  • Clean and safe housing
  • Balanced nutrition
  • Reliable access to clean water
  • Disease prevention and biosecurity
  • Early detection and veterinary support
Prevention first

Building a Healthy Herd

Most animal-health programs begin with management rather than medication.

Clean Housing

Keep shelters dry, well ventilated and protected from excessive heat, cold, rain and drafts.

  • Remove manure regularly.
  • Keep bedding dry.
  • Prevent overcrowding.
  • Repair sharp or dangerous structures.

Balanced Nutrition

Animals need appropriate energy, protein, minerals, vitamins and fibre according to species, age and production stage.

  • Use clean feed.
  • Avoid sudden feed changes.
  • Store feed away from moisture and pests.
  • Provide species-appropriate minerals.

Clean Water

Water is essential for digestion, temperature regulation, growth, milk production and general health.

  • Provide water continuously.
  • Clean drinkers frequently.
  • Prevent manure contamination.
  • Check water quality when problems occur.

Vaccination

Vaccines help protect animals against specific infectious diseases when used correctly.

  • Follow a local veterinary vaccination schedule.
  • Respect storage requirements.
  • Record vaccination dates.
  • Do not vaccinate visibly sick animals without veterinary advice.

Parasite Control

Internal and external parasites can reduce growth, production and animal condition.

  • Monitor body condition.
  • Inspect skin and coat.
  • Use veterinary-guided treatment.
  • Avoid unnecessary repeated drug use.

Early Detection

Farmers are often the first people to notice changes in animal behaviour or production.

  • Observe animals every day.
  • Separate suspicious animals when appropriate.
  • Record unusual deaths or symptoms.
  • Seek veterinary help early.
Farm protection

Biosecurity: Stop Disease Before It Spreads

Biosecurity means reducing the chance that disease enters, survives or spreads within a farm.

01

Control Visitors

Limit unnecessary visitors and avoid allowing contaminated footwear or equipment into animal areas.

02

Quarantine New Animals

Keep newly purchased or returning animals separate while their health status is assessed.

03

Separate Sick Animals

Isolate animals showing suspicious signs when practical and seek professional advice.

04

Clean Equipment

Clean and disinfect suitable equipment, housing and transport areas using appropriate procedures.

Know the warning signs

Common Signs of Illness

A single sign does not always identify a disease. Look for changes from the animal's normal behaviour.

Sign What You May Notice Possible Concern Action
Behaviour Isolation, depression or unusual aggression Illness, pain, stress Observe closely and seek advice if persistent
Appetite Reduced or complete loss of appetite Digestive, infectious or metabolic problems Check feed, water and other symptoms
Breathing Coughing, nasal discharge or difficult breathing Respiratory disease Separate suspicious animals and contact a veterinarian
Digestive Diarrhoea, bloating or abnormal faeces Infection, parasites or feed problems Check water/feed and seek professional advice
Movement Lameness, weakness or reluctance to move Injury, infection or nutritional problems Inspect safely and seek veterinary help
Production Sudden fall in milk, eggs, growth or weight Disease, nutrition or environmental stress Investigate quickly
Species guide

Health Priorities by Animal

Different species have different health risks and management requirements.

🐄

Cattle Health

Good cattle health depends on nutrition, clean water, housing, pasture management, parasite control and disease prevention.

  • Monitor body condition and appetite.
  • Watch for coughing, diarrhoea and nasal discharge.
  • Check hooves and movement regularly.
  • Monitor calves carefully for diarrhoea and weakness.
  • Follow locally recommended vaccination programs.
🐐

Goat & Sheep Health

Small ruminants can be affected by parasites, respiratory problems, nutritional deficiencies and infectious diseases.

  • Monitor body condition and coat quality.
  • Watch for diarrhoea and weakness.
  • Inspect feet and hooves.
  • Manage grazing to reduce parasite exposure.
  • Provide appropriate minerals and clean water.
🐖

Pig Health

Pig health is strongly influenced by hygiene, ventilation, nutrition, stocking density and disease prevention.

  • Keep pens dry and clean.
  • Avoid overcrowding.
  • Provide sufficient clean water.
  • Monitor coughing, diarrhoea and skin changes.
  • Use strict biosecurity when introducing pigs.
🐔

Poultry Health

Poultry require good ventilation, clean litter, safe feed, clean water and strong disease-prevention practices.

  • Watch flock behaviour every day.
  • Remove dead birds promptly and safely.
  • Keep drinkers clean.
  • Control rodents and wild birds.
  • Follow veterinary vaccination programs.
🐇

Rabbit Health

Rabbits need clean housing, good ventilation, suitable forage, balanced feed and careful monitoring.

  • Keep cages dry and clean.
  • Provide appropriate fibre-rich feed.
  • Monitor appetite and droppings.
  • Check eyes, nose, skin and feet.
  • Prevent overcrowding and excessive heat.
🐄

Young Animal Health

Young animals are particularly vulnerable to dehydration, infections, parasites and poor nutrition.

  • Ensure adequate early nutrition.
  • Keep young animals warm, dry and clean.
  • Monitor diarrhoea closely.
  • Observe growth and behaviour.
  • Follow veterinary preventive-care plans.
Disease awareness

Common Health Problems

These examples are for awareness. Diagnosis should be based on clinical examination and, when necessary, laboratory testing.

Problem Animals Commonly Affected Possible Signs Prevention Focus
Internal parasites Cattle, goats, sheep and other livestock Weight loss, weakness, diarrhoea or poor growth Pasture management, monitoring and veterinary-guided control
Respiratory disease Many livestock species Coughing, nasal discharge, breathing difficulty Ventilation, hygiene, reduced stress and biosecurity
Diarrhoeal disease Especially young animals Loose faeces, dehydration, weakness Clean water, hygiene, nutrition and early intervention
Mastitis Dairy animals Abnormal milk, swollen or painful udder Good milking hygiene and udder management
Foot problems Cattle, sheep, goats and pigs Lameness, reluctance to walk Clean/dry housing and regular inspection
External parasites Many livestock species Itching, skin damage, poor coat condition Hygiene, inspection and appropriate control
Prevention program

Vaccination & Preventive Care

Vaccination schedules differ according to species, disease risk, age, location and national veterinary recommendations.

Stage What to Monitor Recommended Practice
New animals Origin, health history and visible signs Use a quarantine and health-assessment process appropriate to the farm
Young animals Growth, appetite, diarrhoea and weakness Follow species-specific veterinary preventive-care schedules
Growing animals Weight gain, parasites and respiratory signs Monitor regularly and maintain good nutrition and housing
Adult animals Production, body condition and reproductive health Maintain vaccination and parasite-control programs according to local advice
Breeding animals Fertility, pregnancy and body condition Use veterinary guidance for reproductive-health planning
Parasite management

Controlling Internal & External Parasites

Parasite control should be based on the species, local risk, season, management system and professional advice.

Internal Parasites

Worms and other internal parasites may interfere with nutrition and growth.

  • Monitor body condition.
  • Watch for poor growth.
  • Use appropriate diagnostic methods where available.
  • Follow veterinary-guided treatment.

External Parasites

Ticks, lice, mites and other parasites can affect skin, comfort and productivity.

  • Inspect skin and coat.
  • Keep housing clean.
  • Reduce parasite habitats.
  • Use approved treatments correctly.

Responsible Treatment

Repeated or incorrect use of medicines can contribute to treatment failure and antimicrobial resistance.

  • Use veterinary guidance.
  • Follow the product label.
  • Respect withdrawal periods.
  • Never guess medication doses.
Act quickly

When to Contact a Veterinarian

Some conditions can deteriorate rapidly. Early professional assessment can be important.

Urgent Warning Signs

  • Severe difficulty breathing
  • Sudden inability to stand
  • Severe bleeding or serious injury
  • Convulsions or severe neurological signs
  • Severe bloating
  • Repeated sudden deaths in a group
  • Large unexplained decline in production

Protect the Rest of the Herd

  • Do not move sick animals unnecessarily.
  • Separate suspicious animals where appropriate.
  • Limit unnecessary visitors.
  • Clean contaminated equipment safely.
  • Record symptoms and deaths.
  • Contact the appropriate veterinary authority.
Farm routine

Simple Animal Health Checklist

A consistent observation routine helps farmers notice problems before they become larger losses.

Daily

  • Observe behaviour
  • Check appetite
  • Check water
  • Look for injuries
  • Check unusual faeces
  • Observe breathing

Weekly

  • Inspect housing thoroughly
  • Check body condition
  • Inspect skin and coat
  • Review feed storage
  • Check equipment
  • Review health records

Regularly

  • Review vaccination records
  • Review parasite-control plan
  • Evaluate animal performance
  • Inspect facilities
  • Update farm records
  • Consult veterinary professionals

Healthy Animals Build Stronger Farms

Animal health is a continuous process. Observe your animals, maintain good management practices, keep accurate records and seek qualified veterinary support whenever a problem is suspected.
