Sheep Farming

A practical guide to sheep production covering breeds, housing, feeding, breeding, lamb care, wool, meat, health, farm management and sustainable production.

Agriculture › Livestock Farming › Ruminant Farming › Sheep Farming

Healthy Sheep. Better Management. Stronger Farms.

Sheep farming can provide meat, wool, milk, breeding animals and manure while making productive use of suitable pasture and farm resources. Successful production depends on good nutrition, housing, health care and responsible management.

What Is Sheep Farming?

Sheep farming is the raising and management of sheep for products such as meat, wool, milk, breeding stock and manure.

Sheep can be raised under grazing, semi-intensive or intensive systems depending on land, climate, feed resources, labor and production goals.

Main Types of Sheep Production

Meat Production

Focuses on healthy growth, good body condition, efficient feeding and quality carcass production.

Wool Production

Uses sheep selected for fleece quality, wool yield and appropriate fiber characteristics.

Breeding Stock

Selects healthy animals with desirable genetics, fertility, structure and production characteristics.

Sheep Breeds and Selection

Breed selection should be based on the farm's production goal, climate, available feed and local market conditions.

  • Select healthy animals with good body structure.
  • Choose breeds that can adapt to the local environment.
  • Consider growth rate and reproductive performance.
  • For wool production, evaluate fleece quality and quantity.
  • For meat production, consider growth and body conformation.
  • Avoid animals with obvious chronic health or structural problems.
Production Goal Important Selection Traits
Meat Growth, body structure, body condition and feed efficiency
Wool Fleece quality, fiber characteristics and wool yield
Breeding Fertility, health, structure and desirable genetics

Sheep Housing and Shelter

Sheep need shelter that protects them from excessive rain, cold, heat, predators and poor environmental conditions.

  • Keep housing dry and clean.
  • Provide adequate ventilation.
  • Avoid overcrowding.
  • Provide comfortable resting areas.
  • Keep feed and water areas clean.
  • Maintain secure fencing around grazing areas.
  • Separate sick or vulnerable animals when necessary.

Climate and Environment

Sheep performance is influenced by temperature, rainfall, humidity, pasture conditions and shelter quality.

  • Provide shade during periods of high heat.
  • Protect sheep from prolonged wet conditions.
  • Keep bedding dry.
  • Ensure good air movement without harmful drafts.
  • Monitor lambs and weak animals closely during difficult weather.

Feeding and Nutrition

Sheep require adequate energy, protein, minerals, vitamins, fiber and water for maintenance, growth, reproduction and production.

  • Provide good-quality pasture, forage or hay.
  • Use supplementary feeds when forage quality is insufficient.
  • Provide appropriate minerals for the local production system.
  • Introduce major feed changes gradually.
  • Prevent access to moldy or spoiled feed.
  • Adjust nutrition according to age and production stage.

Lambs

Need appropriate early nutrition and close monitoring during the first weeks of life.

Pregnant Ewes

Nutritional needs increase during pregnancy, particularly toward the final stage.

Lactating Ewes

Need adequate energy, protein, minerals and water to support milk production.

Water Management

Clean water is essential for digestion, metabolism, temperature regulation and production.

  • Provide clean drinking water every day.
  • Clean water containers regularly.
  • Protect water from manure and contamination.
  • Pay special attention to water availability during hot weather.
  • Monitor animals for signs of dehydration.

Lamb Care and Management

Good newborn management can strongly influence lamb survival, growth and future productivity.

  • Ensure newborn lambs receive adequate colostrum promptly.
  • Keep lambing areas clean and dry.
  • Monitor the navel and general condition after birth.
  • Observe feeding behavior carefully.
  • Protect lambs from cold, excessive wetness and predators.
  • Monitor growth and health regularly.

Breeding and Reproduction

Planned breeding helps maintain a healthy flock and improve reproductive and production performance.

  • Select healthy breeding rams and ewes.
  • Maintain appropriate body condition before breeding.
  • Keep accurate breeding records.
  • Monitor pregnancy and lambing.
  • Evaluate reproductive performance over time.

Wool Production and Management

In wool-producing systems, proper flock management helps maintain fleece quality and animal welfare.

  • Keep sheep clean and free from excessive contamination.
  • Plan shearing according to local conditions and animal needs.
  • Use safe handling techniques during shearing.
  • Store wool in a clean, dry environment.
  • Keep records of wool yield and quality where relevant.

Meat Sheep Management

Meat production depends on healthy growth, appropriate nutrition, good flock health and responsible handling.

  • Monitor body weight and condition.
  • Provide adequate forage and supplementary feed when needed.
  • Control parasites and disease.
  • Reduce unnecessary stress during handling.
  • Plan marketing according to animal condition and market demand.

Sheep Health and Disease Prevention

Regular observation and preventive management are important for maintaining flock health.

  • Check sheep every day.
  • Maintain clean housing and feeding equipment.
  • Follow a locally appropriate vaccination program.
  • Use responsible parasite-control practices.
  • Quarantine newly introduced animals when appropriate.
  • Seek veterinary assistance when serious disease is suspected.
Important: Severe weakness, difficulty breathing, persistent diarrhea, inability to stand, sudden deaths or other serious symptoms require prompt veterinary attention.

Parasite Management

Internal and external parasites can reduce growth, fertility and overall flock productivity.

  • Monitor sheep for poor body condition.
  • Watch for diarrhea and reduced appetite.
  • Inspect the skin and fleece regularly.
  • Avoid unnecessary repeated use of the same parasite-control product.
  • Use veterinary guidance and local recommendations where possible.
  • Manage grazing areas responsibly to reduce parasite pressure.

Biosecurity

Biosecurity helps reduce the introduction and spread of infectious diseases within a flock.

  • Quarantine newly purchased sheep before mixing.
  • Separate sick animals.
  • Clean and disinfect equipment when appropriate.
  • Control unnecessary animal movement.
  • Maintain clean feeding and watering areas.
  • Keep health and treatment records.

Manure and Waste Management

Sheep manure can be recycled as a valuable source of nutrients for crops when handled correctly.

  • Keep manure away from drinking-water sources.
  • Clean housing regularly.
  • Compost manure where appropriate.
  • Use properly managed manure to improve soil fertility.
  • Prevent waste accumulation around animal areas.

Growth and Body Condition

Regular monitoring helps identify nutritional or health problems before they become severe.

  • Record weights where practical.
  • Monitor body condition regularly.
  • Compare growth between animals of similar age.
  • Investigate unexplained weight loss.
  • Give additional attention to young, pregnant and lactating animals.

Sheep Farm Records

Accurate records help farmers understand flock performance and make informed management decisions.

  • Animal identification
  • Birth and lambing dates
  • Breeding information
  • Vaccination and treatment records
  • Deaths and causes where known
  • Growth and weight records
  • Wool production where applicable
  • Feed costs and other major expenses
  • Sales and market information

Sustainable Sheep Farming

Sustainable sheep farming protects natural resources while maintaining healthy animals and productive land.

  • Avoid excessive grazing pressure.
  • Use rotational or managed grazing where practical.
  • Protect water sources.
  • Maintain pasture and forage resources.
  • Recycle manure responsibly.
  • Keep flock size appropriate for available resources.

Sheep Farm Economics

Understanding costs and returns is important for building a sustainable sheep enterprise.

Main Costs

Animals, feed, housing, veterinary care, labor, equipment and transportation.

Possible Income

Meat, wool, breeding animals, milk and manure, depending on the production system.

Important Records

Track expenses, animal performance, production, sales and market information.

Daily Sheep Farm Checklist

  • Check all sheep for signs of illness or unusual behavior.
  • Confirm clean water is available.
  • Check feed and pasture conditions.
  • Inspect fencing and housing.
  • Check lambs and pregnant ewes carefully.
  • Remove dangerous objects from animal areas.
  • Record important health and production observations.

Warning Signs That Need Attention

Farmers should investigate sheep showing signs such as:

  • Loss of appetite
  • Severe weakness
  • Persistent diarrhea
  • Difficulty breathing
  • Abnormal walking
  • Rapid weight loss
  • Unusual swelling
  • Abnormal discharge
  • Sudden changes in flock behavior
Remember: Early detection can help reduce losses. When a serious condition is suspected, contact a qualified veterinary professional promptly.

Golden Rules of Sheep Farming

Start with healthy and well-selected animals.
Provide clean water every day.
Provide balanced nutrition according to production stage.
Keep housing clean, dry and well ventilated.
Monitor sheep every day.
Maintain accurate flock records.
Use responsible parasite and disease-control practices.
Protect pasture, water and other natural resources.
