01 · INTRODUCTION

Understanding Turkey Production

The turkey is a domesticated poultry species raised mainly for meat production. Commercial turkey production is an important branch of intensive poultry farming because turkeys grow rapidly, produce a large quantity of high-quality meat, and can be managed efficiently under controlled housing systems.

Domestic turkeys originated in North America and are now produced commercially in many countries.

Classification

  • Kingdom: Animalia
  • Phylum: Chordata
  • Class: Aves
  • Order: Galliformes
  • Family: Phasianidae
  • Genus: Meleagris
  • Species: Meleagris gallopavo
02 · TYPES

Types of Turkey

Broad-Breasted Types

These are the major commercial meat turkeys.

  • Very rapid growth
  • Large breast muscles
  • High meat yield
  • Efficient feed conversion
  • White feathers are common in commercial strains
  • Natural mating may become difficult in very large birds

Heritage or Traditional Types

These are generally slower-growing birds.

  • Better natural mating ability
  • Greater activity
  • Lower growth rate
  • Lower meat yield than modern commercial strains
  • Suitable for some small-scale or specialty systems
03 · PRODUCTION

Production Systems

Intensive System

Birds are kept in controlled housing with close management of:

  • Feeding and water
  • Temperature
  • Ventilation
  • Lighting
  • Stocking density
  • Disease prevention and biosecurity

Semi-Intensive System

Birds have access to housing and sometimes an outdoor area. It requires less environmental control than a fully intensive system.

Extensive / Free-Range

Birds have greater access to outdoor areas and depend more on natural environmental resources. Growth is generally slower.

04 · PRODUCTION CYCLE

Turkey Production Cycle

01

Breeding

Healthy adult breeding turkeys are maintained to produce fertile eggs through proper nutrition, lighting, body-weight management, and health control.

02

Egg Production

Hatching eggs should be collected frequently, handled carefully, stored appropriately, and prepared for incubation.

03

Incubation

Turkey eggs are artificially incubated. The incubation period is approximately 28 days.

04

Hatching

Newly hatched poults should be dry, active, healthy-looking, able to walk normally, and able to access feed and water.

05

Brooding

Young poults require additional heat because they cannot regulate body temperature effectively when very young.

06

Growing & Finishing

Birds are gradually moved through grower and finisher stages with the objective of achieving healthy growth and suitable market weight.

05 · HOUSING

Housing of Turkeys

A good turkey house should protect birds from environmental hazards while providing adequate space, ventilation, lighting, feed, water, drainage, and dry litter.

✓ Protection from rain and excessive heat
✓ Protection from predators
✓ Adequate ventilation
✓ Appropriate temperature
✓ Adequate floor space
✓ Good lighting
✓ Clean drinking water
✓ Easy access to feed
✓ Good drainage
✓ Dry litter

Litter Management

Commercial turkeys are commonly raised on litter-covered floors. Suitable materials include wood shavings, chopped straw, and other absorbent materials.

Wet litter can contribute to footpad problems, breast lesions, ammonia accumulation, poor welfare, and increased disease risk.

06 · ENVIRONMENT

Temperature & Ventilation

Too Cold

  • Huddling together
  • Excessive chirping
  • Reduced activity
  • Reduced feed and water intake

Too Hot

  • Moving away from heat sources
  • Panting
  • Increased water consumption
  • Wings held away from the body
  • Reduced activity

Ventilation

Good ventilation removes excess moisture, carbon dioxide, ammonia, dust, and excess heat while supplying fresh oxygen.

Poor ventilation can contribute to respiratory disease, wet litter, high ammonia levels, poor growth, and increased mortality.

07 · NUTRITION

Feeding & Nutrition

Turkeys have high nutritional requirements because of their rapid growth. Their diets must provide adequate energy, protein, amino acids, minerals, vitamins, essential fatty acids, and water.

Feeding Phase
Main Purpose
Starter
Supports early growth and tissue development.
Grower
Supports continued body growth and efficient feed utilization.
Finisher
Supports final growth, body condition, and market weight.

Important Amino Acids

Lysine Methionine Threonine Tryptophan Arginine

Water

Turkeys should have continuous access to clean, safe drinking water. Water requirements increase with high environmental temperature, increased feed intake, and increasing body weight.

08 · FEED EFFICIENCY

Feed Conversion

FCR = Feed consumed ÷ Body weight gained

A lower FCR generally indicates more efficient feed utilization.

Feed efficiency can be affected by:

✓ Feed quality

✓ Disease
✓ Water availability
✓ Stocking density
✓ Management
✓ Age
✓ Feed wastage

10 · HEALTH

Common Diseases of Turkeys

Histomoniasis

Blackhead disease

Can cause depression, reduced appetite, weight loss, diarrhea, and high mortality in susceptible flocks.

Newcastle Disease

A viral disease that can affect the respiratory, digestive, and nervous systems.

Avian Influenza

Caused by influenza A viruses. Disease severity varies by virus strain.

Fowl Cholera

A bacterial disease caused by Pasteurella multocida. Possible signs include sudden death, depression, appetite loss, and respiratory signs.

Coccidiosis

Caused by protozoan parasites of the genus Eimeria. It can cause intestinal damage, diarrhea, poor growth, and reduced feed efficiency.

Aspergillosis

A fungal disease associated with inhalation of Aspergillus spores, particularly affecting young birds exposed to contaminated environments.

11 · BIOSECURITY

Disease Prevention & Biosecurity

Disease prevention is usually more economical than treatment. Strong biosecurity reduces the opportunity for pathogens to enter and spread through a flock.

✓ Restrict unnecessary visitors
✓ Control vehicle movement
✓ Use clean protective clothing
✓ Clean and disinfect equipment
✓ Control rodents and insects
✓ Keep wild birds away
✓ Maintain clean water systems
✓ Dispose of dead birds properly
✓ Clean and disinfect houses between groups where practical

Veterinary Guidance

Vaccination and disease-control programs should follow veterinary guidance because recommendations vary according to location, disease risk, and production system.

12 · STOCKING

Stocking Density

Stocking density refers to the number or total weight of birds kept within a given floor area.

✓ Competition for feed and water
✓ Poor air quality
✓ Wet litter
✓ Reduced movement
✓ Increased injuries
✓ Poor growth
✓ Increased disease risk

Appropriate density depends on bird age, final body weight, housing design, ventilation capacity, climate, litter condition, and management system.

13 · CLIMATE

Heat & Cold Stress

☀️ Heat Stress

Important particularly in hot climates.

  • Panting
  • Increased water consumption
  • Reduced feed intake
  • Wings held away from body
  • Reduced activity
  • Poor growth

❄️ Cold Stress

Young poults are particularly vulnerable.

  • Huddling
  • Reduced activity
  • Poor access to feed and water
  • Reduced growth
  • Increased mortality in severe conditions

Management Response

Heat stress can be managed through improved ventilation, sufficient cool drinking water, reduced heat accumulation, appropriate stocking density, suitable feeding schedules, and cooling systems where appropriate.

Cold stress requires adequate brooding and prevention of excessive drafts, especially for young poults.

17 · ECONOMICS

Advantages & Challenges

Advantages

  • Rapid growth
  • High meat production
  • Controlled feeding
  • Better environmental control
  • Easier monitoring
  • High potential productivity
  • Uniform birds
  • Efficient use of labor when well organized

Challenges

  • High initial investment
  • High feed costs
  • Sensitivity to management errors
  • Rapid disease spread in dense populations

  • Heat stress can be serious
  • Waste management is necessary
  • Heavy strains can have mobility and reproduction challenges

QUICK SUMMARY

Turkey Farming at a Glance

Turkey production is the rearing of domesticated turkeys primarily for meat. Commercial production involves breeding, incubation, brooding, growing, finishing, and processing.

Successful production depends on good genetics, balanced nutrition, clean water, proper housing, temperature and ventilation control, biosecurity, disease prevention, appropriate stocking density, and good record keeping.
